# Purpose Workbook: who Krish is, and what he actually wants

Distilled from `Krish scoring workbook 2026 v3 (mindmaker and ikigai).xlsx`
(prepared 6–11 August 2026, companion to KRISH-PORTFOLIO-DECISION-2026.md).
This document is the committed, durable form of that workbook's ikigai and
synthesis sheets, so the theory survives outside any one session or file
upload. Sheet references are kept so a claim can be traced.

The workbook's own caveat applies to everything here: scores are judgements,
not measurements. Treat them as a structured argument that can be disagreed
with. The rankings, however, are his rankings, verbatim, from two 36-question
batches (8 and 11 August 2026). Where the two batches disagree, batch two
wins, because it was answered after seeing the first.

The app-facing slice of this document lives in `src/content/focusTheory.ts`
(the decision rules, the purpose lines). Edit here first, then reflect there.

## 1. The four contradictions to hold (`Ikigai - About Krish`)

1. **Teaching versus repetition.** Best possible day: taught someone (A2
   rank 1). Biggest drain of all: repeating yourself (A3 rank 1). Teaching at
   scale IS repetition, so any workshop, course or cohort business fails on
   his own answer by month four. The only resolution found: a system that
   carries the repetition. That is what CTRL is for.
2. **Commercial instinct versus selling.** Biggest real advantage: commercial
   instinct (B5 rank 1). Most dreaded activity: selling it (A6 rank 1); the
   first customer conversation ranks last among things looked forward to
   (A5). He is strongest at exactly the thing he avoids. Plans requiring
   sustained outbound will not happen; warm intros only (E5 rank 1).
3. **Stated edge versus chosen strategy.** Foresight wins four times: tell me
   what's coming (B1), pattern recognition (B2), you saw it earlier (B3), you
   changed how I think (B4). Technical range is LAST in both B2 and B5. Yet
   twelve months were spent solo building, which uses the lowest-ranked asset
   and leaves the highest-ranked one unmonetised.
4. **Ambition versus the nearest risk.** Proudest outcome: sold a company
   (C1 rank 1). Running out of money ranked LAST of four failures (C2), while
   runway is under 3 months (D2) and the income floor is over 20k a month
   (D1). The nearest risk is ranked least painful. The single biggest blind
   spot in the profile.

## 3. The seven decision rules (`Ikigai - About Krish`)

Anything proposed for Krish gets tested against these before anything else.
They power the "Test an idea" surface on the Focus & Purpose tab.

1. If it needs him to say the same thing twice, it is wrong. (Kills courses,
   cohorts, workshops, recurring facilitation, support-heavy products.)
2. If it needs cold outbound, it will not happen. (Warm intros and published
   thinking only; both already available to him.)
3. If it does not pay inside 90 days, it cannot be the main thing. (It can be
   the parallel asset. It cannot be the plan.)
4. If he is alone in it, expect it to stall. (Co-founder, collective,
   consortium or client teams.)
5. If it only produces income and never ownership, it is a job with extra
   steps. (Sold a company / owns it while someone else runs it is the
   proudest outcome. Fee-only work never gets there.)
6. If it does not build public authority as a by-product, it wastes his top
   regret. (Becoming a genuine public voice is what he would most regret
   never trying, F6 rank 1.)
7. Maintenance is a cost, not a feature. (Maintaining what he built is his
   second biggest drain and fourth dread.)

**The sentence that explains the last twelve months (his own words, D3):**
"I kept building and not selling properly, because I was trying to keep my
options open and have loads of ventures running simultaneously. I want short
term cash desperately but want passive compounding wealth and assets."

## 4. What he wants (batch two, `IKIGAI - what krish wants`)

- **Problem worth five years:** displaced workers have no path forward (A1).
  "People pay for AI and get nothing" ranked LAST, despite being his angry
  prose answer.
- **The real failure:** they do not know what to ask. A SKILLS gap, above a
  data gap (A2, A3).
- **Who:** young people with no network; people about to be displaced (B1,
  B6). Global, English speaking (B3). 100,000 people meaningfully (B5).
- **Success for one person:** earned money they would not have earned; a
  skill they own outright (C1, C3). It feels like a coach (C4). They receive
  proof of what they can do (C6).
- **The form:** a media property with an audience (D1). He owns the data
  layer (D4). Free to start, paid when it works (D5). AI is the enabler, the
  product is the outcome; AI-native ranked LAST (D6).
- **Visibility:** visible at the start, invisible later (F1). In five years:
  own it, but someone else runs it (F6; "have sold it" fell to third from
  batch one's first. Ownership and freedom, not necessarily an exit).
- **Money versus mission (F3):** he would trade only A LITTLE financial
  outcome for mission fit. Money matters more than the mission framing
  suggested.
- **The tension to design around:** he wants to spend his days around
  operators and builders, students LAST (E5), while the people he wants to
  serve are young or displaced (B1, B6). Different rooms. Resolutions: serve
  individuals while paid by organisations, or put a media layer between him
  and the user.

## 5. The wealth-route synthesis (`Wealth Routes`, 11 August 2026)

Four routes scored on six gates; one gate decided every route: **G4, the
collection barrier**.

- Audience without proprietary collection sells for 3–4x profit; with it,
  8–11x EBITDA.
- Equity-for-services and acquisition both KILLED as stated (oversupplied
  delivery, no capital, arithmetic fails).
- Proprietary data: highest ceiling, hardest gate. Sincera sold for
  $25–30m; SemiAnalysis went ~$20m (2025) to a projected $100m+ (2026) on
  supply-chain data, not commentary.

**The answer to the original question:** what puts someone on the right side
of an AI wealth transfer is **owning a collection nobody else can assemble**.
Audience, equity and acquisition are delivery mechanisms for that; treating
any of them as the asset produces a 3x business at best.

**The structural rule underneath:** things getting cheaper: code, analysis,
content, design, advice. Things getting scarcer: distribution, trust,
proprietary data, customer relationships, energy and compute. Every venture
in the existing portfolio sells something getting cheaper.

**The shape that survives** (`Specific Ideas`): the engagement is the
collection mechanism, the collection is the asset, the audience is the
distribution for both. One business, not three; cash arrives in weeks while
ownership accumulates.
